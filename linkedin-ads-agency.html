LinkedIn Ads Agency

LinkedIn ads, when the deal size carries them.

LinkedIn has the best professional targeting of any platform and the highest cost per lead. Both are true at once. The job is knowing when that trade is worth making, and running it properly when it is.

Ex-GroupM & WPP team·$50M+ media managed·Reddit Certified Partner

The short version. LinkedIn works when your average contract value can absorb a cost per lead several times what you would pay elsewhere. When it cannot, no amount of optimisation fixes it, and the honest answer is a different channel.

The trade LinkedIn asks you to make

Nowhere else can you target by job title, seniority, company size, industry and a list of named accounts at the same time, and have it be accurate. That precision is real and it is the reason LinkedIn keeps a place in B2B plans.

It is also why it is expensive. On an account we run in financial services, LinkedIn came in at roughly 2.5x the cost per lead of the same campaign on Meta. That is not a failure of execution. That is the platform.

So the question is never "is LinkedIn expensive". It is whether the leads are enough better to justify it. Sometimes they clearly are, because the people you reach are the buying committee rather than an approximation of it. Often they are not, and we say so.

Where LinkedIn earns its cost

Named account targeting. If you sell into a defined list of companies, matched audiences against your CRM and target account list is genuinely hard to replicate anywhere else.

Buying committees, not buyers. Enterprise deals get decided by several people with different jobs. LinkedIn is the only channel where you can reach all of them deliberately.

High ACV, long cycle. When one closed deal pays for a quarter of media, a high cost per lead stops mattering. Below that threshold it matters a great deal.

Content that gets saved. Document and carousel formats get read and kept in a way feed ads do not, which suits products that need explaining.

Where it does not

Self-serve products under a few thousand a year. Anything with a short cycle and a single decision-maker. Broad consumer audiences, obviously. And any account where the same budget on Reddit would reach the same technical buyers at a fraction of the click cost, which for developer tools, security and infrastructure is common.

That last one is worth being blunt about, because it is the recommendation we make most often. For technical B2B audiences, CPCs on Reddit routinely land well below LinkedIn for people who are actively researching rather than passively scrolling.

How we run it

Tight audiences, not broad ones. LinkedIn's cost structure punishes waste more than any other platform, so audience definition does more work here than anywhere else.

Creative that respects the context. People are on LinkedIn in work mode. Ads that acknowledge that outperform ads that borrow consumer tactics, and the difference is not subtle.

Lead forms against landing pages, tested. Native forms convert better and produce weaker leads. Landing pages produce fewer and better. Which one wins depends on what your sales team can absorb, and it is worth actually testing rather than assuming.

Measurement that survives the cycle. B2B deals close months after the click. Any measurement setup that cannot follow that long a path will tell you LinkedIn failed when it did not, or succeeded when it did not.

Our process

The Signal Method, adapted for a channel where each test costs more.

Days 1–2, baseline. Account audit, audience definition against your actual customer list, and an honest read on whether the numbers can work.

Days 3–5, volume. Creative batch built for the professional feed. Fewer assets than we would run on Meta, because each impression costs more and the testing has to be sharper.

Days 6–7, isolate. Live with a controlled structure. On LinkedIn the discipline matters more, because you cannot afford to learn slowly.

Week 2 onward, amplify. Scale what works, cut what does not, and keep checking the cost per lead against what a closed deal is actually worth.

Who this is for

Good fit. B2B SaaS with a defined ICP and an ACV that carries the cost. Companies selling into named accounts. Teams who care about pipeline quality over lead volume and can tell the difference in their CRM.

Not a fit. Products under a few thousand a year. Anyone wanting cheap leads, where this is the wrong channel by definition. Brands who have not worked out what a qualified lead is worth to them, because without that number there is no way to judge whether LinkedIn is working.

Results

We run LinkedIn alongside Reddit for B2B SaaS. On a cybersecurity account the Reddit side produced 74 qualified leads at $157 each across $11,663 of spend, which is the comparison we usually put in front of clients weighing the two channels. On a financial services account, LinkedIn ran at roughly 2.5x Meta's cost per lead, with a best-performing campaign at CA$59 per lead.

Frequently asked questions

Why is LinkedIn so much more expensive?

Because the targeting is accurate and the inventory is limited. On one account we run, LinkedIn came in at roughly 2.5x the cost per lead of the same campaign on Meta. That is the platform, not the execution, and the question is whether the leads are enough better to justify it.

What ACV do we need for LinkedIn to make sense?

There is no universal number, but the test is simple: work out what a closed deal is worth and how many leads it takes to get one. If that maths survives a cost per lead several times what you pay elsewhere, LinkedIn works. If it does not, we will point you at a cheaper channel.

Lead gen forms or landing pages?

Both, tested. Native forms convert better and produce weaker leads. Landing pages produce fewer and better. Which wins depends on what your sales team can absorb, so it is worth testing rather than assuming.

Should we run LinkedIn or Reddit for technical buyers?

Usually Reddit first, for developer tools, security and infrastructure. CPCs run well below LinkedIn for the same audiences and the people there are actively researching. LinkedIn earns its place when you need named accounts or a full buying committee.

How long before we can judge it?

Longer than other channels. B2B deals close months after the click, so a 30-day read tells you about cost per lead and almost nothing about pipeline quality. Plan on 90 days before drawing conclusions.
