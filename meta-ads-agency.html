Meta Ads Agency

Meta ads, run on creative volume.

Most Meta accounts are not held back by targeting. They are held back by running four creatives a quarter and hoping one of them is the winner. We produce up to 40 a batch, find the angle that works, and put the budget behind it.

Ex-GroupM & WPP team·$50M+ media managed·Reddit Certified Partner

The short version. Meta still works. What stopped working is the way most brands feed it. The algorithm got better at finding buyers for a given asset, which means the asset is now the variable that decides everything. If you are running a handful of creatives and watching CPA climb every quarter, the channel is not the problem.

Why Meta ads stopped responding to the old levers

For years you could fix a Meta account by restructuring it. Split the ad sets, layer the interests, rebuild the lookalikes, tighten the exclusions. That work mattered because the targeting layer was doing the heavy lifting.

It does not any more. Advantage+ and broad targeting have pushed most of the decision-making into delivery, and Meta is now better than you are at finding the person likely to convert on a given ad. What it cannot do is decide whether the ad is any good.

So the lever moved. The accounts that improve are the ones producing enough different ideas to give the system something worth distributing. The accounts that stall are producing one hero asset a month and adjusting audiences around it.

Here is what that looks like in practice. On a B2C SaaS account we tested seven angles against the same audience with the same budget. The best came in at $32 a signup. The worst came in at $79. Same product, same offer, same targeting. The only variable was the idea, and there was no way to know which was which until we ran them.

That 2.4x spread is the entire argument for volume. You cannot reason your way to the winning angle in a planning doc. You have to buy the information.

What we do differently on Meta

We produce the creative. Not a strategy deck telling you what to produce. Up to 40 assets a batch across static, video and UGC-style, built for the placement rather than resized from a brand asset. Feed, Stories, Reels and catalog all reward different things and a single master file cut four ways performs like a single master file cut four ways.

We test angles, not variations. Swapping a button colour is not a test. Testing "this saves you 30 hours a week" against "your competitor already switched" against a customer quote is a test, because those are three different arguments and the market only agrees with one of them.

We fix the measurement before we spend. Browser-side tracking under-reports conversions badly enough that healthy campaigns look mediocre and get cut. The Conversions API is the first thing we set up on a new account, because every decision after that depends on the numbers being real.

We kill things quickly. Losers get cut on a schedule, not when someone gets round to it. That sounds obvious and it is the single most common thing missing from accounts we inherit.

The first three things we fix on an inherited account

Almost every account we take over has the same three problems, in roughly the same order of cost.

1. Placements quietly draining budget. Audience Network is the usual culprit. It is on by default inside Advantage+ placements, it spends real money, and on several accounts we have audited it produced zero purchases over months. It is not a rounding error, it is often a double-digit percentage of spend going nowhere.

2. Attribution set to something nobody chose. Accounts default to a window that flatters or punishes the wrong campaigns. A 7-day-click, 1-day-view setting tells a very different story from 1-day-click, and teams routinely compare reports built on different windows without noticing. Pick one, understand what it is counting, and read everything against it.

3. No server-side tracking. If you are running browser pixel only, you are making decisions on incomplete data. The gap is not small and it is not evenly distributed, so it does not cancel out.

None of this is clever. It is just unglamorous enough that it does not get done.

Our Meta ads process

The same four steps we run on every channel, which we call the Signal Method. Volume in, signal out.

Days 1–2, baseline. Account audit, competitor creative review, positioning. We look at what has already run and what it earned, because the failures in your account history are the cheapest research available.

Days 3–5, volume. First creative batch. Up to 40 assets across formats and hooks, every one tied to a specific angle rather than a design variation.

Days 6–7, isolate. Live with a proper test structure. Baseline established inside 72 hours. Nothing runs blind and nothing runs unmeasured.

Week 2 onward, amplify. Scale what wins, cut what does not, and produce the next batch off what the data just told you. New angles every cycle, informed by the last one.

What good Meta creative looks like now

The first second carries everything. Not the first three seconds. Whatever is on screen when the feed stops moving decides whether anything else gets read.

Native beats polished. The assets that hold attention look like they belong in the feed, not like they escaped from a brand campaign. That does not mean low quality, it means the format is right.

Specificity beats adjectives. "Cut 30 hours of manual ops to zero" outperforms "streamline your workflow" every time, because one of them is a claim you can picture and the other is a category description.

UGC and studio fail differently. Running both is not redundancy, it is coverage. They lose in different conditions, which means the split teaches you something a single format cannot.

Catalog and collection ads are underused. For DTC with real SKU depth, a well-built product feed does work that no single static will, and most accounts we see have either not set it up or set it up once and never looked again.

Meta versus the other channels we run

We run Meta alongside Reddit, TikTok and LinkedIn, and they are not interchangeable.

Meta is the volume channel. Best creative testing environment of any platform, deepest targeting pool, and the fastest place to learn what your market actually responds to. If you only run one paid channel, it is usually this one.

TikTok rewards a different kind of asset entirely and punishes anything that looks like an ad. Lower CPMs, higher creative burn rate. Strong for DTC, harder for considered B2B purchases.

Reddit reaches people mid-research rather than mid-scroll. CPCs run well below LinkedIn for the same technical B2B audiences, and the creative has to read as a contribution rather than an interruption. This is the channel we are certified in.

LinkedIn has the most precise professional targeting and the highest cost per lead of the four. It earns its place when the deal size carries it and rarely when it does not.

The honest answer on channel mix is that most brands under $5M are better off running one channel properly than three badly.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on Meta ads to get a real read?

Plan for $3,000 to $5,000 a month over 60 days. Below that you cannot run enough variants to separate a winning angle from a losing one, and delivery never leaves the learning phase. The budget is not buying reach so much as buying enough conversions for the optimisation to work.

How many creative variants do I actually need?

More than you are running. Seven angles on one account produced a 2.4x spread in cost per signup with nothing else changed. We produce up to 40 assets a batch for that reason.

Do you write the creative or do we?

We produce it. Some clients keep making their own UGC alongside what we make, which works well, because the two sources fail in different conditions and the split teaches you more than either alone.

Should we turn on Advantage+ and the AI enhancements?

Usually, but not before the creative is right. Advantage+ finds the audience for the asset you gave it, so a weak asset just reaches the wrong people more efficiently. Get the angle working first, then consolidate.

What is the first thing you fix on an inherited account?

Placements, then tracking. Audience Network is the most common silent leak. Then the Conversions API, because every decision after that depends on the numbers being real.

How long before we see results?

First ads live in a week. A meaningful read on angles takes about 30 days. A stable, scaled account takes 60 to 90.
