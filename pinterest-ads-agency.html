Pinterest Ads Agency

Pinterest ads, planned around the season.

Pinterest is the one social platform where people arrive already planning to buy something. The catch is that they are planning it months ahead, which breaks every campaign calendar built for Meta.

Ex-GroupM & WPP team·$50M+ media managed·Reddit Certified Partner

The short version. Pinterest behaves less like a social feed and more like a visual search engine with a long memory. Intent is high, competition is thinner than Meta, and the timing is completely different. Brands that treat it like another paid social channel get mediocre results and conclude the platform does not work.

What makes Pinterest different

People are searching, not scrolling. The dominant behaviour is looking for something specific: an outfit, a room, a gift, a recipe. That is much closer to Google than to Instagram, and it means your creative is competing on relevance to a query rather than on interrupting entertainment.

Content has a long life. A Meta ad is effectively dead in days. A Pin keeps being surfaced, saved and clicked for months, which changes how you think about what you put into the platform. The asset you publish in August is still working in November.

The planning window is months, not weeks. This is the one that catches brands out. People plan Christmas in October, spring wardrobes in February, weddings a year ahead. If you launch seasonal campaigns when you would launch them on Meta, you have already missed it.

Saves matter as much as clicks. A save is someone filing your product for later. It is a weaker signal than a purchase and a much stronger one than a scroll-past, and it is worth reading as a performance indicator rather than a vanity metric.

How we run it

Creative built vertical and readable. Pinterest is a portrait-format, detail-heavy environment. Assets that survive being seen small and in a grid perform, and assets that need to be full-screen do not.

Seasonal calendars built backwards. We work back from when your buyer starts planning, not from when they buy. For most DTC categories that means being live six to twelve weeks earlier than the equivalent Meta push.

Feed and catalog properly configured. For eCommerce, Shopping campaigns depend on the product feed being right. Titles, attributes and image quality do more work here than bid adjustments.

Keyword and interest layers together. The platform supports both, and the combination beats either alone. This is where the search-engine comparison stops being a metaphor.

Who Pinterest works for

Good fit. Home, interiors and furniture. Fashion and accessories. Beauty. Food and drink. Weddings and events. Anything gift-shaped. Broadly, categories where the product is visual and the purchase is planned rather than impulsive.

Not a fit. B2B software, almost always. Anything urgent or problem-triggered, where the buyer is not browsing ahead. Products that photograph poorly or need explanation before they make sense.

Pinterest in a wider mix

Pinterest rarely carries an account on its own and works well as the planning-stage layer underneath channels that close.

Meta does the volume and the testing. TikTok does discovery for a younger audience. Google Shopping catches the person who has already decided. Pinterest reaches them weeks or months before any of those, while the decision is still being formed, which is also why attribution flatters the later channels and understates this one.

That last point is worth planning for. If you judge Pinterest on last-click you will underrate it, because by the time someone buys they have usually arrived through something else.

Who this is for

Good fit. DTC brands in visual categories with a real product feed. Brands with clear seasonal peaks who can plan months ahead. Anyone whose Meta costs have climbed to the point where a thinner auction is worth exploring.

Not a fit. B2B. Brands needing revenue this month, because the timing does not work that way. Anyone who cannot produce portrait creative, since repurposed square assets underperform badly here.

Results

We run Pinterest inside multi-channel DTC programmes alongside Meta, Google and Reddit, including home retail and lifestyle accounts. +114% year on year store revenue for a gifting brand across channels. $50M+ in paid media managed by the team across WPP and GroupM.

Frequently asked questions

When should we launch seasonal Pinterest campaigns?

Six to twelve weeks earlier than the equivalent Meta campaign. People plan Christmas in October and spring wardrobes in February. Launching on a Meta calendar means arriving after the decision has been made.

Does Pinterest work for B2B?

Almost never, and we will say so rather than take the budget. The platform rewards visual, planned, consumer purchases. B2B software is the wrong shape for it in nearly every case.

Can we reuse our Meta creative?

Not well. Pinterest is portrait-format and detail-heavy, and square assets from Meta get cropped or shown small in a grid where they stop being readable. Creative built for the platform is not optional here.

How should we measure Pinterest?

Not on last-click alone, or you will underrate it. Pinterest reaches people while the decision is forming, so by the time they buy they have usually arrived through another channel. Saves are a real signal worth tracking alongside conversions.

How much budget does it need?

Less than Meta for a meaningful test, because the auction is thinner. $2,000 to $3,000 a month over a season is usually enough to tell whether the category works, provided the creative is built for the platform.
